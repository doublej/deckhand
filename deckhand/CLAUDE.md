# The app

## What this is

The iOS app target: `App`, `Model`, the recorders and the screens under `Views/`. Anything with
no UIKit in it belongs in `DeckhandKit` instead, where `just test` can reach it.

The target is generated by XcodeGen from `../project.yml` — **there is no checked-in Info.plist
to edit**; change `project.yml` and run `just generate`.

## Mental model

`Views/Manager/` is the manager panel and everything about it, including its own `AppModel`
extension — `deckhand/` is on the 16-file gate, so `ManagerDesk.swift` holds the state object and
the model's half of it together, the way `Views/Rail/Linking.swift` already does.

`Model` holds the listing and the selection; the files beside it are one feature each
(`Dictating`, `Controlling`, `Filtering`, `Keeping`, `Markups`, `Reading`, `Snaps`,
`LiveUpdates`). `Views/` is the screens, `Views/Dashboard/` the grid a session opens on,
`Views/Session/` the session page's parts,
`Views/Artifacts/` slips,
`Views/Annotation/` marking up a picture (its own packet — read it before touching that folder),
`Views/Archive/` every clip this phone has recorded and the one player that sounds any of them —
`Listening` is that player's transport, drawn for a sound file the Files tab opens,
`Views/Talk/` the mark itself,
`Views/Reading/` the one turn a hold opens and the text view that makes it selectable,
`Views/Settings/` the parts of the settings page that are more than a row — today the Macs
found on this network.
`Views/Artifacts/AloudCards.swift` is the other way a slip can be drawn — cards with the app's own
talk mark, from a page that declared its questions (`Aloud`); `ArtifactView` is the one place that
chooses between it and the web view. `Views/Projects/` is the picker that starts a new session; its state is `ProjectPicker`, in
`Starting.swift` rather than four more properties on `AppModel`, which sits at its 300-line gate.

## Invariants

- **No clip is ever the only copy in `tmp`.** A clip used to live in the temporary directory
  until its upload succeeded, and the upload ran inside `run`, which swallows a throw — so a
  sleeping Mac, a refused token or a timeout left the file there unreferenced and the words
  nowhere at all. Every clip is now filed by `ClipArchive` *before* it goes on the wire
  (`Keeping.swift`), and every outcome is written back onto its entry. Nothing in the app deletes
  a clip: only the sweep, which drops what actually reached a session after seven days, and
  the person, from the archive screen. A clip cut off *mid*-recording is still lost and always
  will be — AAC writes its index on stop, so an unfinished clip is unplayable bytes anywhere.
  `heldClips` is the join from a review card back to the clips its words came from, and it
  is *replaced* on a fresh clip exactly as `hold` replaces the card.
- **What a clip's menu offers is gated on the audio, never on the words.** Copying and "Send to…"
  need a transcript and were once wrapped around the whole menu, so a clip whose transcription
  failed — the exact clip the archive exists for — was left with nothing but Delete. "Try again
  on…" sits outside that check and asks `ClipArchive.canRetry`: audio still on disk, and not
  already delivered. Not `failure != nil`, which would miss the clip whose upload never answered
  at all, and not "always", which would offer a confusing second send on a clip that arrived.
  `AppModel.tryAgain` re-uploads through `deliver`, so the same bookkeeping writes the outcome —
  a second failure lands on the row the way the first one did. Its phone-side spans are honestly
  zero except `spoken`, carried over from the original: nothing was armed, recorded or filed this
  time. And the entry is retargeted at the moment the session is chosen, not when it lands, or a
  failed retry would leave the row naming a dead session while the red line beneath it talked
  about the live one.
- **The clock starts where a press becomes a recording, not where a finger lands.** `TalkGesture`
  waits a fixed 200 ms before a press counts as talking at all, and no gesture on this phone can
  report the moment a touch began — so `Recorder.spans.arming` measures everything after the
  settle (permission and `AVAudioSession.setActive` are both in it, and both are real) and the
  settle itself is named in the doc comment rather than guessed at. The one span that cannot be
  closed where it is opened is the last: `AppModel.answeredAt` is set when the Mac answers and
  `SessionFoot`'s `.onAppear` closes it, because "the card is on screen" is a fact only a view
  has. It is answered once per clip — the card comes back every time somebody swipes between
  panes, and only the first of those is the one the person waited for.
- **A clip's breakdown is shown twice, drawn once, never in a sheet and no longer in the card.**
  `TimingsView` is the drawing; the clip archive expands a row into it, and the review card's
  round-trip line (`ClipCost`) pulls it down onto a slab of its own beneath the card. It has now
  been in three places and each move was a different failure: a sheet took the card off the screen
  in order to explain the card, and drawing the table *inside* the card doubled the height of the
  thing you were about to send. The slab is on the page, not in the box. Exact milliseconds,
  because a bar with no number cannot answer "which stage and by how much" — the bars behind the
  rows are only there so the answer can be found without reading fourteen numbers first. That line
  is still the one thing allowed to sit below Send: it is a receipt, not a fourth verb, and nothing
  both pressable and consequential goes under the button that sends.
- **The review card's buttons are not in the card, and that is the corner audit's real answer.**
  A 44pt control is a 22pt corner; nested 16pt inside a card whose own corner was 12, the inner
  curve is *wider* than the one containing it, which reads as fighting. Tightening the button to
  `Radius.small` fixed the arithmetic and left a row of small hard rectangles at the foot of a soft
  card. So the nesting went instead: Discard, Edit and Send sit below the container, on the page's
  own ground, nested in nothing — free to be the capsule iOS makes a 44pt control, and the card
  went up to `Radius.extraLarge` because nothing is concentric with it any more. The box now holds
  the prompt and the things that add to it; everything you do *with* a finished prompt is under it.
  A deck column is the exception and keeps all of it inside: 240 points has no room for a row of
  capsules standing on their own.
- **`Rewrites` is the before the card never had, drawn as a diff over the after.** The note said
  *what* the chain swapped; it could not show the sentence those words replaced, so a swap that
  was wrong read as a mishearing and a mishearing read as a swap. It was a whole before-block,
  which asked somebody to read two paragraphs and spot the difference — it is now one paragraph
  with the gone words struck through and the new ones in the card's own ink, off `diffWords`
  (`DeckhandKit/Clips/TextDiff.swift`, where `just test` can prove the ordering). The far end is
  the *prepared* words, never the card's draft: `Filtered` was built about those, so an edit typed
  by hand would otherwise be attributed to a filter that never saw it. utt's own cleanup and this
  Mac's filter chain are two rows rather than one, because the answer to each is in a different
  app. utt's half is nil on every Mac today and draws nothing — the gate is
  `UploadReceipt.uttRewrote`, which asks utt's `stages`
  and never `raw != text`, exactly as utt's own `ProcessedTranscript.changed` does.
- **There is no longer hold to give a second gesture.** A hold of any length already *is* the
  recording, from `TalkGesture.settle` onwards, so the other three things get the press each mode
  does not use: a tap where talking is a hold, a long press where talking is a tap. One
  physical press reaches both, because a release arrives as a tap a moment later — so everything
  goes through `TalkControl.claimed()`, which lets the first gesture claim the touch for
  `sameTouch`. Only `SessionFoot` passes `onReach`: a sessions row spends its tap on opening the
  session and its swipe on starting it, and a deck column spends its own, so neither is handed a
  third meaning on the same touch. That press opens a `confirmationDialog` — Photos, Clipboard,
  Type, Clips, in that order, so the one that was here before is the one nearest the thumb. Type
  opens the review card's `Editor` empty and Done lays the words in the card, never sends — and it must
  stay a dialog: a `.contextMenu` installs a long press of its own and would take the touch from
  the tap under it, which is this same bug for the third time. Nothing says any of it at rest.
- **A gesture on a list row has to share the touch.** The sessions row has three jobs on one
  touch: tap to open, press to talk, swipe to star. `highPriorityGesture` and `ExclusiveGesture`
  both take the touch and broke the tap *and* the swipe; a `Button` takes it the other way and the
  press never fires; a `DragGesture` sequenced after the long press is read as the start of a
  swipe. What works is the plainest thing: not a `Button`, `.onLongPressGesture` for the press
  (with `onPressingChanged` for hold-to-talk's release, which is why there is no drag in it) and
  `.onTapGesture` for the tap, whose meaning `TalkControl.tapped` decides. `UITests` holds all
  three down, including that a press must not open the session.
- **A `NavigationLink` inside a `List` costs two things**: it draws the system's disclosure chevron
  outside the row's own fill, and it swallows `swipeActions` so a swipe never reveals them. The
  sessions list uses a plain `Button` and `navigationDestination(item:)` instead. The UI test
  caught the second one ("no star action on the row"); nothing catches the first.
- **Hiding the navigation bar takes swipe-back with it.** `SessionView` catches its own, and where
  the gesture lives is the load-bearing part. It is a clear 20pt strip **overlaid on the panes**
  (`SessionView.back`), not a `simultaneousGesture` on the page: the panes are a `TabView(.page)`,
  whose paging is a UIKit pan inside the view, so a simultaneous gesture is recognised *beside* it
  and one drag would page and pop at once. An overlay takes the touch before that pan sees it.
  The edge is the **leading** one — `layoutDirection`, not `startLocation.x < 28`, which was the
  wrong side of the screen in a right-to-left reading. The price is that the leading 20pt of a
  pane no longer scroll vertically; the panes are padded wider than that.
- **Ten talk marks on one screen need `isMine`, not ten recorders.** `AloudCards` draws a mark per
  question against the *one* recorder handed down to it, and `TalkControl(isMine:)` is what keeps
  the other nine at rest — the same arrangement the deck uses for its columns. The accent is
  per-card for the same reason: the card being talked into goes utt orange and the rest stay teal,
  which is the only way a screen full of marks says which one is listening.
- **`.textSelection(.enabled)` is not selection on iOS, and putting it back is the regression.**
  On this floor it gives a `Text` one gesture with one meaning — select the whole view and offer
  Copy — with no handles and no way to take a line out of a page. It was already on the turn and
  on the file source, which is exactly what "I can only copy the entire thing" was: the modifier
  working. Range selection is a `UITextView` and nothing else (`Views/Reading/SelectableText`),
  so a hold on a turn opens `TurnReader` and the file viewer's source side is that text view.
  The hold goes on the **words**, never on the card, for the reason every other gesture here is
  written the way it is: a long press applied outside a control takes the touch from it, and the
  card holds the thinking toggle. Where a screen has no reader — the manager panel — the turn
  keeps the modifier rather than growing a second meaning for the same press.
  `TurnReader` shows the **source**, not the rendering: a fenced command only exists there, and a
  selection lifted out of a rendered view pastes back with its markdown stripped.
- **A turn is drawn as the document it is, through `Markdown` rather than `Prose`.** The two
  renderers differ by what their contract is for: `Prose` shows anything outside its four blocks
  as the characters the model typed, because a summary is written against a prompt this repo owns
  and a briefing that broke that contract must look broken. A turn has no contract to break — it
  is whatever a person dictated or another session wrote, and what another session writes is
  markdown. `Prose` is not to be widened to cover it, and a third renderer would be a third place
  the fence rule could drift out of step.
- **A mermaid fence draws, and the seam is the environment.** `Markdown` (in DeckhandKit) knows a
  diagram when it parses one; drawing it needs a web view, which cannot live in that module. So
  the app hands the renderer in at the root with `.diagrams { }` and `Views/Diagrams/MermaidBlock`
  does the work. mermaid is **bundled** (`deckhand/Resources/mermaid.min.js`, MIT, 11.12.3) and
  injected as a `WKUserScript` — host-initiated, the lane the slip bridge already uses — so a
  diagram draws with the Mac asleep and no policy was loosened. A web view has no opinion about
  its own height, so the page measures what mermaid drew and posts it back; a diagram that will
  not parse posts zero and the block falls back to the source lines, which is what every surface
  did before any of this existed. Slips do **not** get this yet: an artifact page is sandboxed by
  its own CSP and nothing injects mermaid into one.
- **The orders pane writes on a sheet, and it has to.** `OrdersView` shows what a session has been
  told to keep in mind and opens `Editor` to change it. It is itself on a sheet now, opened from
  the dashboard's square — which only strengthens the argument below rather than retiring it: the
  field still cannot sit on the pane, and `Editor` is still what a dictated paragraph needs. A field on the pane itself would put a
  second `TalkControl` on a screen that already floats one over it: two controls reading the one
  recorder, both live, one filling a box and the other sending to the session — the `isMine`
  arrangement `AloudCards` uses cannot help, because these two do different things with the same
  clip. A sheet covers the mark, so the only control listening is the one under your thumb, and
  `Editor` already opens focused with the keyboard up. Its own loading rather than an `AppModel`
  extension, because `deckhand/` sits on its 16-file gate and nothing outside that pane wants it.
- **A clip said from the overview is narrated on its own row, because there is nowhere else.** A
  session's page answers "did that go?" twice — the mark narrates the wait in its left-hand pill,
  and the words then land in the transcript under it. The rail and the deck have neither: the row
  *is* the button, and under it is the next session. So `TalkLine` draws the one line those
  surfaces get, and the words in flight are the mark's own — `TalkNarration` was lifted out of
  `TalkMark` for exactly that, because a second copy would eventually promise SEND on one screen
  and PREVIEW on another for the same press. The sentence afterwards is the strip's: `report`
  writes `status` and the row's `Spoken` together, so a row can never be told something the strip
  was not, and the row's copy goes quiet after 30 seconds where the strip's does not need to.
  Nothing at all while a card is up — the card already says where the words are going.
- **Neither overview has a staging area; both hand off to the one that already exists.** The rail
  had the deck column's card — `compact`, 10pt words, the verbs crushed onto one line — on a
  full-width phone, and the answer is not a second full-size copy of the card the session's own
  page already gets right. Both surfaces get `SendOrRefine` instead: send the words as they stand,
  or open the session and work on them there. **Refine carries nothing, and that is why it is
  small.** Held words already live on the model under the session's own id (`review`, with
  `reviewFiltered`, `reviewTiming`, `heldClips` and `attached` beside it) and `SessionFoot` draws
  its card straight out of them — so carrying the clip across *is* `chosen = session`, and the
  card is there when the page arrives with the chain's notes and the round-trip line on it. The
  one thing that surface must still say out loud is a refusal: the card's red line is where that
  normally lives, and without it Send would be a button that quietly does nothing. Discard is not
  offered — it is the answer that loses something, and it belongs beside the words it is losing.
  A full card on the rail was also barred from `more` for the orders-pane reason above (the row
  over it is a live `TalkControl` on the same recorder), which is one more argument against one.
- **Landscape had no way into a session's page, and Refine is why it has one now.** `RootView`
  swaps the deck for the rail on geometry alone, and only the rail held a `NavigationStack` — so
  a column's Refine had nowhere to go. `DeckView` is now a host of its own, and deliberately the
  rail's arrangement rather than a new one: the same `navigationDestination(item:)` pushing the
  same page, with the bar hidden for the same reason. **Rotating while that page is up loses the
  page, not the work.** The rotation swaps the whole screen, so the pushed view goes with it —
  but the words being worked on are model state under the session's id, so the rail offers them
  again the moment it appears. This is the first screen landscape has ever pushed; if a session
  page turns out to be wrong at that height, the host is four lines to take back out.
- **The transcript poll asks for what is on screen, not for a fixed page.** It fetched the newest
  eight every five seconds and replaced the list with them, so a step back into the conversation
  was taken away again before it could be read — which is what "I cannot see the whole transcript
  after a restart" actually was: nothing is kept on the phone and nothing needs to be, the Mac has
  the file and does the parsing (`page_turns`, `?before=`). `AppModel.shown` is the poll's limit
  and it grows with `turns.count`. Two things follow. The auto-scroll watches
  `turns.last?.timestamp`, never `turns.count` — reading further back grows the list at the far
  end, and pinning to the bottom on every growth throws you straight out of where you went. And
  the row ids are positions, so a prepend renumbers everything: `TranscriptView.step` scrolls to
  index `added` afterwards, one runloop hop later, or the reader lands wherever the new page
  begins.
- **The panes are swiped between, and there is no bar at the foot.** A word per pane across every
  screen to choose between those screens was a band of chrome doing what a swipe does for free, so
  `pane` is a `TabView(.page)` and the bar is gone — the panes are that much taller and the talk
  mark sits where the bar was. Its native index display is **off**: those dots land exactly where
  the mark floats. The indicator is a dot per pane on the header's meter row instead, which was
  already being drawn and had the space spare, so the whole change costs nothing and returns a
  band. Two things follow. The dots are not a control — dots you can hit are dots somebody will
  try to hit — and the page's own edge-swipe-to-leave has to be arbitrated against the paging
  rather than restricted to the first pane. It fires from **every** pane, and the strip that
  catches it wins by being drawn over them.
- **There are three panes and all three are things you read: the dashboard, the briefing, the
  transcript.** It was five. Slips, files and orders were three swipes each of which had to be
  *arrived* at before you could learn whether it had anything on it, and a carousel you page past
  on the way to the transcript is the wrong home for something you do once. They are squares now.
  The dashboard opens first because it is where a session starts, and the order here is the swipe
  order, so the leading edge that leaves the page is still the first pane's.
- **A square that cannot carry a number is not a square.** `Views/Dashboard/` is a grid, and every
  tile carries the count it is about — slips waiting, files touched, the first written line of the
  standing orders, how many filters run out of how many this Mac offers, what the CLAUDE.md tree
  costs, how full the window is and what is answering. The name is not one of them — it is the
  page's title, which is where it already was. That is the whole reason it beats the menu
  it replaced, which was nine words with no figures beside any of them. **A square names things;
  it does not draw them**: under the number sit up to three lines of what the number is *of* —
  three slip titles, three filenames, three subagent descriptions, one line of plain text each.
  `ArtifactsView`, `FilesView` and `OrdersView` are untouched and arrive on a sheet, framed with a
  title because all three were written to sit under a header that named the session and would
  otherwise land with none, and none of them is what a tile draws. The rule is enforced
  by what is *not* on the grid — a tile whose only content is a word in a box is a menu row drawn
  four times too large, which is why the raw transcript stayed in the `…` menu: its line count is
  not known until it has been fetched, and a number that arrives only after you press the tile is
  not a number on the tile. What a tile *says* is `DeckhandKit/Dashboard/`, where `just test` can
  prove every sentence; the order they are read in is the order they are written, because a grid's
  reading order is a layout fact rather than a rule anything can check.
- **A sheet does not remove the view that presented it, so the grid reads itself again on the way
  back.** `DashboardView` fills every square from one `load()`, and a sheet leaves the presenter
  in the hierarchy — so `.task` never fires a second time and every tile went stale the moment its
  own sheet changed the thing it counts. Answer a slip and SLIPS kept the old count; add the alias
  that fixes "web" and ON THE WAY IN still said "Nothing changed", which is that square's whole
  demo path ending on the sentence it was rewritten to stop telling. One `onDismiss` on the
  `$opened` sheet covers all twelve. Three things make it work and each was a separate bug.
  `load()` used to latch on an `asked` flag and could only run once, so adding `onDismiss` alone
  changed *nothing* — the latch had to go first. Every assignment replaces a value and never
  clears one, so a failed re-read leaves a square saying the last true thing instead of falling to
  a zero that reads as a fact. And `AppModel.context` answers a refused request with an **empty**
  `ContextDocs` rather than nil, so a plain `docs = await tree` would swap fifteen CLAUDE.md files
  for "No CLAUDE.md" on any re-read that failed; an empty tree is believed only the first time.
  The `$doc` sheet needs none of this — it is declared *inside* the context sheet, so closing a
  document returns to the tree and the reload fires when the tree closes.
- **A tile is as tall as what it says, and no taller.** They were literal squares once, with the
  eyebrow pinned to the top and the number to the bottom and a third of each card empty between
  them — which reads as a card that failed to load, and cost so much room that two and a half rows
  of a twelve-square grid were visible at once. Three things fixed it and the third is the one
  that is easy to undo by accident. The number moved up under the eyebrow. The preview took the
  middle. And **a tile with nothing to preview is short rather than stretched**: filling the row's
  height was tried first and put the void straight back, because ORDERS with nothing standing
  drawn to the height of a FILES square listing three filenames is the same empty card under
  another rule. So the cards hug their content and `GridItem` is `alignment: .top`, which puts
  what is left over *between* squares, where it reads as a gutter. Nothing is padded to a shape,
  and nothing is invented to fill one — a tile with no true line shows none, exactly as a tile
  with no true number says "None yet" rather than "0". This is also why `CONTEXT` lost its
  caption: "Every CLAUDE.md it started with" explains the tile rather than saying anything about
  the session, and it was only ever there because the square had nothing else in it.
- **The subagents square is the one tile that costs nothing and answers the most.** `model.agents`
  is refreshed at the tail of every `loadTurns`, which the session page polls every five seconds
  whichever pane is showing, so the count of what is running is free and always current. Finding a
  subagent used to mean knowing roughly which turn started it and scrolling the transcript to that
  row — `AgentTrace` draws the ones a turn started and `LooseAgents` gathers the running strays,
  and both are readings of the conversation rather than a listing. `AgentList` is the listing, and
  it is the one place that shows **finished** agents too: `LooseAgents` is running-only because a
  permanent roll-call pinned under a transcript is a second transcript, and a square is the exact
  opposite case — it is where a full listing was asked for. The row is `AgentFace` either way,
  which is why that type is no longer private, and the destination is `AgentView` either way.
  `SessionView` clears `model.agents` beside `model.turns`: one session's at a time, and a square
  claiming the last session's count for a second is the one thing this grid must not do.
- **Two of the four controls are on the grid and two are not, and it is the same line the Lock
  Screen card draws.** `cancel` and `compact` only ever make a session do less, so they are
  squares — and compact carries the window figure that decides whether to press it, which is the
  argument for a dashboard in one tile. `clear` and `exit` cannot be undone and they stayed in the
  menu in their own section, red and asking first: a grid of identical squares is where a thumb
  moves fastest, and that is the worst place in the app for a mis-tap that costs something.
- **Asking first and cannot-be-undone are two questions, and `Control` answers them separately.**
  They were one property while the two sets happened to be equal, and the menu filtered on
  `asksFirst` to draw its red section. Compacting now asks — it costs the detail of everything
  said so far — so `isIrreversible` is what that section filters on, and compact stays a square.
  Demoting it into the red group would have said it was `clear`, and putting it on the grid
  ungated was how it got pressed by accident. The dashboard's `run` routes it through
  `SessionView.confirming`, which is the *same* dialog the menu's two use: a control carries its
  own `Caution`, so a second surface needs no second dialog and cannot ask the wrong question.
- **A tile's preview lines are drawn as the kind of thing they are.** They were three lines of
  11pt text whatever was in them, so what kind of thing a square held could only be learned by
  reading it. `TileLook` is the four kinds — a slip draws each title behind a small ruled page,
  because a slip *is* an HTML page; subagents and queued prompts draw in mono behind a prompt
  chevron; files and CLAUDE.md documents draw in mono behind a `doc`; everything else, being
  prose somebody wrote, stays plain. It changes the **line and never the ground**: nothing draws
  a filled shape inside the card, which is the nesting the tile's own packet forbids, and the one
  drawn thing (`PageMark`) is a glyph at glyph scale with no fill, right on both surfaces.
- **The session's name is the page's title and the title is the rename.** It was a `NAME` square
  on the grid, printing the same name `SessionHead` already draws in the same 20pt two rows above
  it — two copies of one word, and the pressable one was the lower. The title carries a pencil
  now and opens the same `Renaming` sheet; the square, the `.name` destination and the page's
  `rename` are gone. The pencil is **absent** rather than dimmed where `Session.renames` is false
  — a Codex thread, or a Claude session with no pane: `/rename` is typed into a pane, and a strip has nowhere to put the square's
  "Needs its pane" caption. `manager/doing.py`'s `PANES` never knew the word, so nothing on the
  wire lost a name it could ask for.
- **The manager still knows five pane names and the phone still answers to all five.**
  `manager/doing.py`'s `PANES` is unchanged and is not the app's to change. `SessionView.show`
  maps "summary" and "transcript" onto panes and "slips", "files" and "orders" onto the dashboard
  with that square already open — so the square is held by the *page* (`SessionView.square`) and
  not by the pane, because the ask can land before anybody has looked at the dashboard. A phone
  that quietly stopped answering to one of those names would be a tool reporting success and
  doing nothing.
- **The session header is a name and a menu, and everything else is the row under it.** The
  chevron and the glyph tile both went: the panes are swiped between and a swipe from the leading
  edge of any of them leaves the session, so the arrow was a second way to do what the page
  already does, and a session's glyph tells one row from another in a *list* of them — on the
  session's own page there is only one session. The way out is still named in the menu, because
  a gesture nobody is told about is not a way out for somebody using VoiceOver. Under the name
  sits everything that is true of the session rather than something you press: the window as a
  13pt ring (a bar ran the width of the page to say one number, and read as the page's progress
  rather than the session's), the count, BUSY or IDLE, and the pane dots. The relative time is
  drawn **only when idle** — `updated_at` is the age of an idle session and nothing else.
- **A swipe names where it landed, then stops.** The dots say how many panes there are; they
  cannot say which one this is, and a swipe can be accidental. So the pane's name slides in at
  the top and leaves after a second. It takes no touch and it must never become permanent — a
  label that stayed would be the tab bar again under another name.
- **A control that fires a request has to say so where the thumb is.** The slip's send button
  answered, delivered, and looked dead: the only receipt was in the bar at the top of the screen
  and the button is at the foot of a long scroll. It got pressed again, and every press was
  another answer into the session. So it says "Sending…", it refuses a second tap while one is
  in flight, and success closes the screen — a web slip closes itself with `deckhand.close()`,
  and anything the app draws in place of a page has to do that job itself.
- **A sequenced gesture eats the tap beside it, and it does so silently.** `.gesture(LongPress
  .sequenced(before: DragGesture(minimumDistance: 0)))` with `.onTapGesture` outside it claims the
  touch on the way *down*, so the tap never fires however short it is — no error, no warning, the
  handler simply never runs. That was `TalkGesture`'s `.hold` branch, which is the default mode,
  which means the clip archive was unreachable from a session's page for as long as it was written
  that way, and the three-option menu that replaced it inherited the silence. `.onLongPressGesture`
  + `.onTapGesture` is the pairing that works — a list row has always used it for this exact job.
  Both `.hold` branches are one case now. Two UI tests hold it down, one per mode, and the
  *difference* between them is the diagnostic: the menu worked on a long press in tap-to-talk and
  did nothing on a tap in hold-to-talk, which is what narrowed a screenful of suspects to one line.
  Neither a titled dialog nor giving each presentation its own host changed anything, so four
  presentations on one view is measured and fine rather than merely feared.
- **Two gestures on one control means one modifier, and the order inside it is the bug.** The
  archive gesture went on as a second `ViewModifier` wrapping `TalkGesture`, which put its long
  press *outside* the tap — and a long press applied outside a tap takes the touch, so
  tap-to-talk stopped working entirely while hold-to-talk was fine. Every working pair in this
  file is long press **inner**, tap **outer**; they are in one modifier now so the order cannot
  drift apart again. The release of a long press also arrives as a tap a moment later, and in
  tap-to-talk a tap starts recording — so letting go claims the touch, or reading your clips
  would open the microphone behind them.
- **The mark says nothing at rest, and that includes the archive.** A standing "· HOLD FOR CLIPS"
  was tried as a footnote to the gesture nobody can discover by trying it, and it was on screen
  every second the app was open to explain a thing you do once. It is gone. Nothing is labelled
  at rest here — the rule has no exception, and the exception is what made it noise.
- **The word changes, the pill does not.** `.id(word)` was on `TalkMark.pill`, so every new word
  tore the whole capsule down — fill, shadow and all — and built another. Invisible while a wait
  said one word; once the wait learned to narrate itself (`ClipNarration`) it was the button
  flashing in and out six times. `WordRoll` is the fix and the *layout* rule inside it is the part
  worth keeping: the incoming word is a hidden `Text` that owns the container's size and the pair
  actually animating hangs off it as an overlay, out of layout entirely — so the capsule makes one
  smooth resize from the first frame rather than settling after the letters land, and a staggered
  letter arrives where it will finally sit instead of sliding into place. The mark cannot be
  shoved either way: each wing is a `maxWidth: .infinity` half and the pill grows about its own
  centre inside it. Two styles, switched by `@AppStorage("talk.letters")` and a row in Settings —
  a design question with an answer coming, not a preference worth putting in `Preferences`.
- **The talk control is a mark and nothing else.** It floats over the pane rather than sitting
  under it, and the scrolling panes add `TalkMark.clearance` to the end of their content so
  nothing is stuck behind it. No bar, no ground, no resting label: a band of furniture across the
  page to hold one button was the first thing asked to go. What a tap will do slides out on a pill
  to the left, the way out to the right, and both the mark and that left pill send — the pill
  reads as an instruction otherwise. Because it floats, `TalkControl` sets **no** `contentShape`
  of its own: the hit area is whatever the caller drew, or an invisible full-width slot would
  swallow taps meant for the page behind it. Nothing is labelled at rest, so the UI test looks for
  the mark's `talk-mark` identifier.
- **Reading it first is on by default.** Speaking into a busy session cannot be undone, so the
  transcript is held in a card — utt's own review HUD, minus the keycaps a phone has no use for —
  until it is sent or discarded. Off in Settings sends the moment the words come back. A prompt a
  filter stopped goes back into the review card rather than only into the status line: the card is
  the one place words exist unsent and it is editable, which is the only useful answer to "a filter
  will not pass that".
- **Recording pins the phone's orientation** (`OrientationLock`, and the one reason this app has a
  UIApplicationDelegate). Portrait and landscape are two different screens here, so a rotation
  swaps the view holding the recorder and the clip goes with it.
- **A filter's settings are saved on dismissal, not per change.** A text field changes per
  keystroke — the same thing that produced 40-odd revisions in four seconds on the Mac's own
  plugin page — so `onDisappear` covers both Done and a swipe-down, and a dirty flag means no PUT
  when nothing changed.
- **A raw JSONL line cannot be one unwrapped `Text`.** The first raw-transcript view was a single
  `Text` of the whole file inside a `ScrollView([.horizontal, .vertical])`. Unwrapped, a 10k-char
  line is tens of thousands of points wide, and past that CoreText renders **nothing** — a black
  screen, no error, no warning. It is one wrapped `Text` per line in a `LazyVStack` now, and it
  says how many lines arrived so that empty never looks like broken again.
- **A picture is never pinned to nothing.** `AppModel.attach` opens the review card holding just
  the picture when there is not one already. Every caller but the mark's own menu has a card on
  screen by definition, so the guard is dead code for them; without it a picture put up from the
  mark at rest went into `attached` with nowhere to draw it — not lost, since the next clip
  transcribed opens a card already holding it, but off the screen until then, which from the
  outside is a picture that was dropped. An empty card is the surface for talking about a picture
  and it already exists: `SayRest` is on it, a tap on the thumbnail opens the marking sheet, and
  Discard throws the picture away with the words. `hold("")` is still the one door in, so the
  chain's notes stay nil rather than becoming an empty `Filtered` nothing wrote.
- **A `PhotosPicker` cannot be a `Menu` item.** It is a control, not a button, and a menu row built
  from one opens nothing at all. `.photosPicker(isPresented:)` is the same picker with the trigger
  separated from it, which is what a menu needs — `ReviewCard.pictures`.
- **`PhotosPicker` needs no usage description**, and `UIPasteboard.hasImages` needs no permission —
  which is why the clipboard button only appears when there is something to paste: asking
  `UIPasteboard.general.image` is what triggers the system's paste consent, and only then.
- **A busy session cannot be made to read early.** The queue drains when a turn ends, so the
  phone's "force" is Escape and the button says "Stop the run so it reads this".
- **The manager panel is a shell around the root, not a `TabView(.page)` around the rail**, and
  its swipe takes the touch from nothing. Paging a `TabView` is a UIKit pan *inside* the view,
  with no say in which drags it answers to; a clear strip overlaid on the trailing edge — what
  this was, 24pt of it — has the say but costs its own width in vertical scrolling, the same
  price `SessionView.back` pays for 20, and means aiming at an edge to reach a screen.
  `ManagerShell` instead **watches** every drag with `simultaneousGesture` and moves the screen
  for one shape of them: sideways rather than up the page, trailing-going, and only while the
  rail is what you are looking at. Three things make that safe and each is load-bearing.
  **Every row action moved to the leading edge** (star *and* hide), so a leading-going drag is
  the row's and this never answers to one. **A drag up the page is the list's**, which is what
  keeps a full-screen gesture from costing what a strip cost.
  **It stands down while a session's page is up** (`ManagerDesk.covered`, set by `SessionView`) —
  the shell wraps the whole app, so without that it is a third meaning for a sideways drag on a
  screen that already has two, and it silently took the pane paging and the way out with it.
  `ManagerPanelTests` holds one test per clause; they are the design, not decoration.
  `RailFoot` gets a glyph too: a gesture nobody is told about is not a way in for VoiceOver.
- **An `accessibilityIdentifier` on a container renames every element under it.** The manager
  panel wore `manager-panel` on its outermost `VStack`, and SwiftUI pushed that name down onto
  the text field, both buttons and the talk mark — every one of them came back *called*
  `manager-panel`, so `manager-field` and `manager-send` matched nothing and the panel read as a
  screen with no controls on it. The failure is silent and the error says "no matches", which
  points at the identifier that is missing rather than at the one that ate it. Identifiers go on
  leaves. The panel is recognised in tests by its field, which exists on that screen and no other.
- **The shell sits *outside* `RootView`'s `.ignoresSafeArea(.keyboard)`, deliberately.** The rail
  and the deck ignore the keyboard because nothing on them is typed into. The panel is one long
  field and has to rise for it, which it cannot do inside an ancestor that ignores it.
- **What the manager asks of the phone lands in two slots, not one.** The rail owns which session
  is pushed and the session's own page owns which pane shows — two gates, two views, so
  `ManagerDesk` holds `route` and `pane` separately. One slot loses the first of them whenever a
  run asks for both, which is exactly what "open deckhand and show me its slips" is, and it was
  the first thing the manager actually did. The session page reads `pane` on the way in as well
  as on a change: both were set before that view existed, so an `onChange` alone never fires.
- **A command is obeyed only when it is newer than the cursor the panel opened with.** Without
  that, every relaunch replays every piece of navigation the manager has ever asked for, in
  order, at launch. `ManagerDesk.obeyFrom` is set once, to whatever the Mac's newest entry was.
- **Nothing said to the manager is held for reading first.** The review card exists because a
  prompt into a busy session cannot be undone; a sentence to the manager can simply be followed
  by another, so `TalkMark(confirms: false)` and the clip goes straight up.
- **A tap on a Lock Screen card was dead for as long as the card has existed.** The widget set
  `.widgetURL(URL(string: "deckhand://waiting"))` and *nothing answered it*: no `CFBundleURLTypes`
  anywhere, no `onOpenURL` anywhere, so every tap on every card opened the app on the sessions
  list. Both halves are needed and neither is visible from the other — the scheme is registered in
  `project.yml` (there is no checked-in Info.plist to put it in), and `App.swift` reads the URL.
  The shape is written once, in `DeckhandKit/LiveActivity.swift`: `Deck.cardLink` for the widget,
  `CardLink.session(openedBy:)` for the app. `CardLink` sits **outside** that file's `#if os(iOS)`
  on purpose — `Deck` needs ActivityKit and cannot exist on the Mac, but a URL is only characters,
  so `just test` holds both ends of it down. Two things it caught: `lastPathComponent` answers
  `"/"` rather than empty for `deckhand://sessions/`, which would send the rail hunting for a
  session called `/`; and the route has to be set **after** a refresh, because a cold launch from
  the Lock Screen arrives before the first listing does and the rail can only push a session it is
  already holding. It goes into `manager.route`, not a second slot beside it — the rail already
  acts on exactly this ask from the manager, and a second way to say "open this session" is a
  second place the answer can drift. Landscape is the deck and has no route handler: a tap there
  lands on the deck, which is every session at once, so there is nothing to push.
- **One card is one session, so a push token has to say which session.** `LiveUpdates` walked
  `Activity<Deck>.activities` and posted a token per card with only its *kind*, and the Mac keeps
  one slot per kind — so the last card to register was the only one the daemon could still reach
  and every other card sat on the Lock Screen never updating again. That is the four stacked cards
  in the screenshot. Each `activity` token now carries `activity.attributes.session`, read once
  outside the token loop because a card never changes which session it speaks for. The
  push-to-start token carries **no session key at all**, not an empty one: it belongs to the app
  rather than to a card, and the daemon's parameter defaults to empty, so absence *is* the start
  slot.
- **The card's one button lives in `widgets/CardAction.swift` and is compiled into both targets.**
  A `LiveActivityIntent` is named by the widget's `Button` and *performed in the app's process*,
  which is the whole reason it is that protocol and not a plain `AppIntent`: the app's process has
  the pairing the person typed in, where a widget extension has a `UserDefaults` of its own and
  would find no Mac. Membership in two targets (`project.yml`) rather than a third module, because
  an App Intent in a Swift package needs `AppIntentsPackage` ceremony for one small file.
  **A card may only ever make a session do less, never more.** Stop is on it because Escape can
  only take work away. There is no Approve and there is not going to be one — answering a
  permission prompt is the keyboard route, which arrives *as the person at the machine*, and a
  card carries one line of what the session said rather than the prompt it is blocked on. No
  `compact`, `clear` or `exit` either: two of them cannot be undone and a Lock Screen has nowhere
  to ask first.
- **A Codex or OpenCode session is offered what the Mac will do for it, and what it will not is
  absent rather than dimmed.** Every view asks a capability on `Session`, never the provider. A
  Codex thread has no pane and no CLAUDE.md tree, so it loses the dashboard's Context, Compact, On
  the Mac and Clear inbox squares, the pencil, the header's Run section, the project page's
  compact and exit, and the review card's Command button — and `AppModel.context` does not ask, or
  the refusal would land in `status`. An OpenCode session keeps Compact, the pencil, Clear inbox
  and the Command button, because OpenCode's API does each without a pane, and loses only Context,
  On the Mac, and clear and exit. Stop stays live for both with no pane: the Mac asks that
  program to interrupt its own turn. The word `CODEX` or `OPENCODE` (`ProviderWord`) sits beside
  the state on the rail row and in the header, and the transcript's eyebrow says it too. A
  project row asks Claude Code, Codex or OpenCode in a `confirmationDialog` every time — never
  remembered.
- **The pairing screen finds the Mac, and never moves one that is working.** `Beacons` (in
  `Views/Settings/`) browses for `_deckhand._tcp` through `DeckhandKit`'s `Lookout` and fills the
  Address field in — the `.local` name when `/health` answers on it at the moment of the tap,
  and the Mac's raw IP when it does not. The row says so while it asks: a tap that costs seconds
  has to admit it, the same rule the slip's send button is written to. The Token field is untouched, because mDNS is an
  unauthenticated broadcast and discovery finds an address rather than a secret. Auto-filling is `ServerSettings.adopts` and
  nothing else: an address nobody has typed, one still on the built-in default, or one this phone
  has just failed to reach — so two Macs on one network cannot quietly swap under a pairing that
  answers. `Connection.answering` is `Bool?` for that rule, and the nil matters: a check in flight
  and a missing token are both "nobody has asked", not "it failed".
- **Three silences, three sentences.** A browse that has not finished, one that heard nothing, and
  one this phone refused to run are `Looking`'s separate cases, and the row says a different thing
  for each — a refused local network permission finds nothing *silently* on iOS, so collapsing
  them would print "no Mac here" at somebody whose phone simply would not look. Turning that
  permission on is Settings › Deckhand › Local Network, and the row says so.
- **ATS:** the app talks plain HTTP to a `.local` name, which needs `NSAllowsLocalNetworking` plus
  `NSLocalNetworkUsageDescription` — both in `project.yml`, not a checked-in plist.

## Running it on hardware

- `just sim` builds, installs and launches in the simulator, already paired.
- `just device` (`just phone="Pocketline Swing" device`) takes four things, and the error names
  whichever is missing: Xcode signed in to the account for team KA6433FU8U;
  `-allowProvisioningDeviceRegistration` alongside `-allowProvisioningUpdates`, or a phone not
  already in the team's wildcard profile is refused with "isn't registered in your developer
  account"; an **unlocked** phone, or the developer disk image will not mount; and, the first time
  a phone gets a build from this team, the profile trusted by hand in Settings → General → VPN &
  Device Management. Deployment target is iOS 18.0 — the 12 Pro runs 18.7.
- `xcrun devicectl device process launch ... -- -deckhand.host <host> -deckhand.token <token>`
  starts the app already paired; the model writes launch-argument settings back to defaults, so
  the pairing survives a cold start. The `--` matters: devicectl eats the flags without it.

### Installing on a phone that is somewhere else

`just release` — archive, package, publish to `install.jurrejan.com`, and the phone installs it
from Safari. `just unpublish-release` takes it down again.

**`just device` cannot reach a phone off this network and no VPN fixes it.** Xcode finds a
wireless device over Bonjour, which is link-local multicast; a routed tunnel carries none, and
even reflected it would not help, because iOS advertises `_remotepairing._tcp` on its physical
link and never into a `utun`. Measured on 2026-09-13 with the phone on the house WireGuard:
`devicectl` still said `unavailable` and a 6-second browse found nothing. That road is closed.

**The ordinary development signature installs over the air, and that is the surprise.** Apple
documents this as needing an Ad Hoc or Enterprise profile, and the whole apparatus for one was
built here first — a distribution certificate, which needs the developer portal, which needs an
account, which on a locked Mac needs an App Store Connect key. None of it turned out to be
necessary. Measured on iOS 26: a Release build signed `iOS Team Provisioning Profile:
dev.jurrejan.deckhand`, served with a manifest, installed. Being in the profile's device list
appears to be the whole of what the installer checks.

Three things follow, all of them better than the Ad Hoc build would have been: `aps-environment`
stays `development`, so the daemon goes on pushing to the sandbox APNs host and the Lock Screen
card keeps working; `get-task-allow` stays true, so a debugger still attaches; and nothing
expires before the profile does. The one real limit is that device list — a phone that has never
had a build from this Mac is not in it, and adding one needs Xcode at an unlocked screen.

**The manifest's content type is not optional.** Caddy has no MIME entry for `.plist`, and iOS
reports an empty one as "cannot connect to install.jurrejan.com" — a sentence about the wrong
thing entirely. `cdy site` regenerates the vhost config and drops it, which is why
`publish-release` writes `tools/install.caddy` back every time rather than once.
